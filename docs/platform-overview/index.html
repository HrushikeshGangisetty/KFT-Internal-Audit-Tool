Getting around

Sign in, Home and navigation

Sign in

/login

Company accounts with a server-side session. Nothing that could be stolen from the browser is stored in it.

  • Session cookie is HttpOnly; no tokens in browser storage (checked by the walkthrough).
  • Deactivating a user ends their sessions immediately.

Home for a team member

/

Opens on what is waiting for this person: the big "Report a problem" button, FC lookup, their queue and recent activity.

  • Queue tabs: issues I own, reports to acknowledge, handoffs to answer, my tasks.
  • Shows whether today's update is posted, with a one-click link if not.
  • Home previews six items per tab and links to the full queue.

Home for heads and the manager

/

Adds a Needs attention panel across every team they can see.

  • Lists: unacknowledged past target, escalated, blocked tasks, blocked FCs, no update today.
  • Plain lists with counts. No per-person rankings, by design.
  • FC pipeline shows units per lifecycle stage.

On a phone

Same app on Android or Windows browsers. The menu folds into a drawer; buttons are at least 44 px tall.

  • Menu button opens the drawer; choosing a page closes it.
  • Every screen stacks to one column with no sideways scrolling (checked at 375 px).

Notifications and quick search

The bell shows unread alerts (assignments, acknowledgement deadlines, escalations, approvals). The search box at the top searches every past issue you can see from any page.

  • Opening the bell marks alerts as read.
  • Alerts come from a background worker, so deadlines fire even when nobody is logged in.
Work

Issues: report, own, resolve, verify

An issue can be about anything: a field report, a software bug, a production fault. An FC, stage or version is optional.

Report a problem

/report

Three required things: what the problem is, what you saw, and how urgent it is. Everything else, including photos and logs, is optional.

  • The report is routed to a team queue automatically; the queue head becomes accountable.
  • An acknowledgement deadline starts; missed deadlines escalate.
  • Large touch-friendly urgency choice for phone use on the floor.

My queue

/queue

Everything waiting on you in one place, with the action next to each row.

  • Acknowledge confirms you have seen a report. Ownership and progress are tracked separately.
  • Handoffs: accept, or reject with a reason. The issue never ends up without an accountable owner.
  • Overdue acknowledgements are shown in red.

Issue list

/issues

All issues you may see, with urgency, owner, severity and status at a glance.

  • Filters: full-text, status, severity, category, stage, department.
  • Loads 25 at a time and fetches more as you scroll, so it stays fast as history grows.

Issue detail

/issues/<id>

The full record: what was seen, the investigation, root cause and fix, who verified it, and where it was discovered compared with where it originated.

  • Investigation notes are append-only.
  • Reassignment always needs a reason and is kept in the history.
  • Verification must be done by someone other than the resolver.
  • A head can promote the fix to a known issue; similar past issues are listed alongside.

"Has this happened before?"

/issues/new

While someone types a symptom, matching past issues and known fixes appear beside the form.

  • Matches only what the person is allowed to see.
  • Uses FC, stage, category and version details to rank matches.
Work

Tasks and daily progress

My tasks and today's update

/tasks

Planned work with status, priority and due date, plus a short daily note: progress, next step, blockers.

  • Earlier days' updates are kept as written; only today's can be corrected, and the correction keeps history.
  • Heads can assign tasks to others; members manage their own.

Team progress (heads and manager)

/tasks?view=team

Who has not posted today, blockers and next steps, overdue and blocked tasks, reports past their acknowledgement target, and assigned work.

  • Filter by department. Lists only, no scores.
Releases

Software releases and announcements

A release can only be announced once the Software head and the manager, two different people, have approved the exact current revision.

Release history

/releases

Every GCS and Configurator release with its latest revision and approval state. Old records migrated from the previous "push update" screen are labelled as not approved under the current process.

  • Software members with drafting rights can start a release from here.

Release detail and announcement

/releases/<id>

The revision content, both approvals, and the announcement email.

  • Editing creates a new revision and clears approvals.
  • Preview shows the exact subject, body and recipient list before anything is sent. Internal notes never go into the email.
  • Send works once, even on a double click. Each recipient's delivery is tracked; failed ones can be retried and uncertain ones reconciled.
  • Recipients are synthetic test addresses until the signup API is connected.
Hardware

Flight controllers, firmware and test evidence

Flight controllers

/fcs

Every registered unit with model, revision, stage, status and open issues. Search by serial, revision or batch.

  • Serials are assigned automatically (FC-YYYY-NNNNN) when a unit is registered.

FC detail

/fcs/<id>

The unit's whole life: the 11 lifecycle stages, timeline, issues, firmware flashed, test results and audit.

  • A stage cannot pass while it has unverified blocking issues.
  • Only the manager can approve or reject at the final stage; rejecting asks for confirmation.
  • "Report an issue" pre-fills the FC and stage.

Stage records and rework

Every attempt at every stage. A failed stage can record rework and send the unit back to an allowed earlier stage.

  • Rework links to the issue that caused it, so the timeline tells the full story.

Register a flight controller

/fcs/new

Model, hardware revision, PCB batch. The unit starts at Fabrication.

  • Shown only to production roles.

Test & evidence: parameter compare

/param-compare

Compare two .param dumps: changed values, parameters on one side only, grouped and filterable, with a tolerance for float noise.

  • Dumps can be stored against an FC to keep approved, flashed and read-back configuration apart.

Firmware catalogue

/firmware/builds

The builds that can be flashed: version, source, signing, bootloader, scripts. Maintained by the Firmware team.

  • Flashing copies the build's details onto the FC record, so history stays accurate if the catalogue changes.
  • Builds are retired, not deleted.
Knowledge

Known issues, search and activity

Known issues

/known-issues

Curated entries promoted from resolved issues: symptoms, root cause, fix, affected versions and how often it has occurred.

  • Occurrences open on demand and respect access rules.

Knowledge search

/search

Full-text search over symptoms, root causes and fixes with structured filters for stage, category, and hardware, firmware, GCS and Configurator versions.

  • Matching known issues are shown above raw results.

Activity log

/audit

Append-only record of changes with before and after values. The application and database refuse edits and deletes.

  • Shows only changes to records the viewer may see.
Configuration

Test checklists, FC models and accounts

Test configuration (manager)

/manager/test-configuration

The checklists testers answer at each test stage.

  • Every change bumps the checklist version; completed tests keep the exact items they answered.
  • Items already answered can be disabled but not removed.

FC models (manager)

/manager/fc-models

Models offered when registering a unit. Archived, never deleted, so existing units stay valid.

  • Archiving asks for confirmation first.

Accounts (admin)

/admin

Users and roles, departments, and the GCS/Configurator version list used on test records.

  • Role changes update the matching permissions and are audited.
How it fits together

Key workflows

Highlighted steps are control points the system enforces.

A reported problem

Report→ Routed to team queue→ Acknowledge by deadline→ Investigate (notes, handoffs)→ Resolve with root cause→ Verified by a different person→ Close→ Promote to known issue

Missed acknowledgement deadlines escalate. A handoff changes the owner only when the receiving team accepts.

A flight controller

Register→ Assembly, soldering, QC→ Firmware flash→ Sensor, bench, ground, final tests→ Manager approval

A failed stage raises an issue; rework returns the unit to an allowed earlier stage. Blocking issues must be resolved and verified before a stage can pass.

A software release

Draft revision→ Software head approves+ Manager approves→ Preview email→ Send once→ Track each delivery

Any edit creates a new revision that needs both approvals again. Sending announces a release; it does not install it.

Controls

Safeguards built in

  • Server-side access checks on every list, search, count, linked record, attachment and export path. Hiding a button is never the control.
  • Two-person rules: release approval needs two different people; fix verification needs someone other than the resolver.
  • Nothing silently lost: append-only notes, updates and activity log; reassignments need a reason; models and builds are archived, not deleted.
  • Evidence kept separate: discovery facts, root cause, approved parameters, flashed configuration and read-back evidence are recorded separately with provenance.
  • Email is deliberate: preview first, one send per draft, per-recipient history. Only synthetic test recipients today.
  • Sessions: HttpOnly cookie and CSRF protection; no credentials in browser storage.
This round

UI rework and testing (20 Oct 2026)

What changed in the interface

  • Each feature is now its own module (issues, tasks, releases, FCs, firmware, knowledge, configuration, admin) that owns its screens and routes.
  • Screens load on first visit, so the app stays fast as features are added.
  • Long lists load 25 at a time as you scroll, with a "Showing N of M" count.
  • In-app dialogs replace browser pop-ups for reasons and confirmations; short confirmations appear as toasts.
  • Phone drawer menu, search bar on every page, back links on detail pages, subtle animations that switch off when the device asks for reduced motion.
  • Removed duplicate admin screens and fixed several layout bugs.

How it was tested

  • 269 backend tests passed on a disposable local database.
  • Three browser walkthroughs, 49 steps, nine user accounts, on a fresh disposable database: production loop, configuration, and everyday work including releases and the phone layout. No page errors.
  • Permission refusals are checked in the browser as well as in tests (e.g. a technician cannot reassign another team's issue).
  • No shared or live data was touched; no real email was sent.
